Entering a Whole New World

Prologue

A Whole New World

Somewhere across the city, something was on fire.

Not everything - not the way disasters look in movies, where the whole skyline becomes one wall of flame. It was more scattered than that. Pockets of orange glow reflected off low clouds. Smoke rose in three or four columns from different directions, lazy and dark against the night. The sounds were worse than the sight - distant crashes, something shattering, the low hum of chaos that meant whatever was happening down there hadn't finished yet.

I stood at the rooftop's edge with both hands deep in my pants pockets, the torn fabric of my clothes still carrying the smell of wherever we'd just come from, and looked up.

Habit.

When everything around me was going wrong, I looked up. The clouds had that orange tint from the scattered fires below - but they were still just clouds. Still just the sky. There was something grounding about that.

My thumb found the locket beneath my collar without me thinking about it. I felt the shape of it through the fabric - silver, small, barely larger than the tip of a water bottle, with a strange stone set in its centre. I always found myself reaching for it when I needed to think clearly.

I needed to think clearly.

Behind me, a groan.

I turned.

Satoshi was stirring on the rooftop gravel, and even half-unconscious and crumpled on the ground, he looked exactly like himself - messy black hair splayed out in every direction like it had never once in its life agreed to cooperate, his torn shirt twisted across an athletic frame that hadn't quite filled out yet but already showed the shape of what it was going to be. His jaw was set the way it always was when he was in pain but refusing to show it. And then his eyes opened, and there it was - that spark. Even now, dazed and blinking in the firelight, there was something behind his eyes that couldn't be put out. Like electricity looking for somewhere to go.

Something loosened in my chest.

"Thank God," I murmured. Then louder: "Thank God you're awake. I thought you got hit by the Thing back there. Guess it was just an illusion."

Satoshi blinked. His eyes moved across the sky, across the rooftop surface, then settled on my face.

He stared.

"...Do I know you?" Low. Rough.

My expression didn't move. "What?"

"Do I know you?" Louder this time. Sitting up straighter, studying me with no recognition at all - not a flicker of it.

The weight dropped into my chest. I knew that weight.

Of course, I thought. Of course it happened to him too.

A second groan from near the door.

Gotam was pushing himself upright against the rooftop wall. He was smaller than Satoshi - twelve years old, lean and slightly built, his tanned skin smudged with dirt and ash. His clothes were more torn than ours, hanging off him in a way that suggested the battle we'd come from had been less kind to him. He didn't look weak - that wasn't it. He looked like someone still figuring out what his body was going to be, still a chapter or two behind the story. His forehead caught the light when he tilted his head, and his eyes - sharp, analytical, always reading something even when there was nothing to read - moved from me to Satoshi and back with the practiced speed of someone who never stopped taking inventory of a room.

No glasses. People always assumed, but no.

"Where am I?" he said. Then, blinking: "Who are you two?"

I closed my eyes for just a moment.

Here we go again.

I knew how we'd gotten here. Every detail of it - the wormhole opening, the pull, the fall, landing hard on this rooftop in the dark. I remembered all of it. They didn't. And that gap - between what I knew and what they had - was something I'd have to manage carefully.

"Okay," I said, keeping my voice steady. "I know you're both confused. Trust me - I've been through exactly this before. New world. No memories." I turned toward the city and gestured out at it. "But first - look."

They stood. Slowly. And they looked.

The streets below were not empty. But whatever moved through them didn't move like people. It shambled. It lurched. It hunted.

The city was full of zombies.
