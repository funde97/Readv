Entering a Whole New World

Chapter One

The Day of Lies and Truth

"My name is Max."

I told them everything. Or - almost everything.

Four years ago, I was fourteen years old, and my life had already been far from normal. Strange things happened around me. Things I couldn't explain, things I'd long stopped trying to put into words. But none of that had prepared me for the day the WORMHOLE opened - not by accident, not by anyone else - by mine. And then it went wrong.

When I came out the other side - into their world - I was ten. Not older. Younger. Four years stripped from my body like the transit had taken something as payment. I arrived with nothing: no idea where I was, no memory of how I'd gotten there, no one I could trust. My only lifeline had been a single diary I'd somehow held onto through the passage - my own handwriting staring back at me, full of thoughts from a version of myself I could barely remember.

And now here I was. Four years later. Fourteen again - standing on a rooftop on a planet that looked exactly like Earth but wasn't, with two people who had no memory of knowing me, while the city below us smouldered.

They listened without speaking. Then Goh crossed his arms. "So how did we end up here?"

"Good question," I said. "What's the last thing you remember?"

Satoshi's expression shifted. Something moved through his eyes - quick, bright, like excitement catching - and then cut off entirely. He opened his mouth. Stopped.

Silence.

"What?" Goh pressed.

"...Nothing." Satoshi looked away. "I was excited about something. Something that was supposed to happen tomorrow." A pause. "I'm not excited about it anymore."

Tomorrow.

The word dropped into my mind like a stone in still water. I kept my face neutral. The day Satoshi's memory had snapped back to - I knew it. Recognized it without needing to think twice. It was the same day, four years ago, when I had first stumbled out of a wormhole and into their world.

Not a coincidence. I was almost certain.

"Goh?" I asked.

"Nothing dramatic," he said with a shrug. "I was online, reading some research - actually a genuinely fascinating paper on-" And he was off, walking through the argument with the specific energy of someone who had never once considered that now was not the time. For a twelve-year-old, the commitment was almost impressive.

I waited.

Nerds.

"Alright," I said, when he paused for air. "We pick this apart later. Right now I need to know if this building is safe." I moved toward the rooftop door. "I'll go down first. Check the stairwell, check the floors. If it's clear, I come back and knock three times. You two stay here."

"Wait-" Goh started.

I turned back and looked at them both. Calm. Deliberate. "I've relived four years," I said. "Fourteen plus four - that makes me eighteen. Which means I'm the adult here, and the adult goes first."

I watched the words land. Watched confusion ripple across both their faces as their minds tried to find the flaw and came up empty. No memories to anchor themselves. No shared ground to push back from. They couldn't argue with what they couldn't untangle - and right now, that gap was exactly what I needed.

"Three knocks," I said. "Nothing else."

I didn't wait for them to work it out.

✦   ✦   ✦

- Goh's Perspective -

And just like that, he was gone.

I stared at the closed door. Then I turned to Satoshi. "What do you think of him?"

"Max?" He barely had to consider it. "I trust him. Seems decent."

And just like that - the doubt dissolved.

Here's the thing about Satoshi: I trusted his judgment more than I trusted my own instincts. I had no memories to explain it - no history, no evidence, nothing rational to point to. It was just there, deep and immediate, like something that had always been true and was simply waiting for me to remember it. If he looked at someone and decided they were worth trusting, that landed heavier than anything my gut could come up with on its own.

But I'll admit - part of me was still watching the door.

Because Max was strange. Not in a threatening way, exactly. Just - strange. Spiky black hair that somehow held its shape despite everything, a build that was lean and athletic for someone his age, like his body had been specifically designed for situations like this one. He stood differently from how most people stood - hands in his pants pockets, chin tilted up slightly, like the chaos around him was something he'd already accounted for and moved past. The kind of calm that didn't come from not being afraid. The kind that came from having been afraid so many times it stopped meaning anything.

And then there was the locket.

I'd clocked it when he turned toward the door - small, silver, no bigger than the tip of a water bottle, with something dark set in its centre. A stone of some kind. Not decorative. It didn't look like something you wore because it looked good. It looked like something you carried because you had to.

Max was probably hiding something. I still thought so.

Satoshi said he was decent.

Decent it was.

Three sharp knocks.

"Open up. It's me."

I exhaled.

✦   ✦   ✦

- Max's Perspective -

The stairwell was quiet.

Not just quiet. Too quiet. No broken glass, no debris, no marks of whatever was happening outside. The building was completely intact - like it had been lifted from somewhere else and set down here carefully, untouched, while everything around it fell apart.

I stood in the empty corridor and let the silence settle.

Familiar, I thought.

More than familiar. The angle of the stairwell. The shape of the landing. The way the dim light fell through the window at the far end -

I have been here before.

The thought didn't scare me. It worried me, which was different - worse, in its own quiet way.

Here was the strange part: I hadn't lost those four years. Whatever this world had taken from me - and it had taken plenty - it wasn't memory. I knew the exact date my body had rewound to. I could feel the difference between fourteen and ten in my own bones, and I had the diary to prove it.

My father - the one I'd gained on this side of the wormhole, not the one I'd left behind - had given it to me for my tenth birthday. That birthday, that gift, was the very first thing I remembered after waking up in this world. Whatever came before it - the fall, the wormhole itself, the actual moment of arriving - was gone, sealed shut behind a door I'd never found the handle to. But everything after the diary, I had. Four years and three months of it, by the date of the last entry, which lined up close enough with "fourteen, again" that I trusted the math.

The diary itself didn't say much. A school I'd changed. A couple of medals. And then - nothing, for one year and seven months. Not a single entry. After that gap, it started again, right around my thirteenth birthday, like someone had picked the pen back up after finally deciding something.

I had a guess about what that something was.

I wasn't ready to look at it straight on yet.

But I was fairly sure this building was tangled up in it somehow. There was no sign of a struggle here - no broken glass, no smoke stains, nothing that matched a single thing happening four floors below us and getting worse by the minute outside the windows. It was as if someone had lifted this whole structure out of somewhere else and set it down gently in the middle of the wreckage, on purpose, untouched.

I shook the thought loose. Later. Three knocks - that was the deal I'd made, and I went to keep it.

I climbed back up and rapped my knuckles against the rooftop door three times. It opened before I'd even lowered my hand - Goh, apparently, had been counting.

"Well?" he said.

"Clear. Quiet, too. Quieter than it has any right to be."

That earned me a look I didn't love - the kind Goh probably gave to math problems that refused to add up. I let it go and led them down.

Going first wasn't only about being the so-called adult in the room, either, though that's the reason I'd given them. There was a smaller, quieter one underneath it that I hadn't said out loud to anyone: if something in this building had teeth, I wanted it to find me before it found them. It felt like the least I could do, measured against whatever I'd done to leave a hole in my own memory shaped exactly like guilt.

The building gave up its floors one at a time, and every one of them told the same story: nothing. The fifteenth floor was a maze of glass-walled offices, chairs spun half around like someone had stood up mid-sentence and never come back to finish it. The fourteenth had a small kitchen, and the coffee pot inside it was still warm enough to fog the glass - whatever had happened here, it hadn't happened long ago. The thirteenth was carpet and cubicles and a corkboard with someone's kid's crayon drawing pinned up beside a meeting schedule and a stranger's smiling face under the words EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH.

Goh's pace slowed with every floor we cleared. He wasn't built for this kind of moving - all that brain, not a lot left over for stairs - and by the time we hit twelve, he'd stopped pretending otherwise. Satoshi, meanwhile, had upgraded from bored to aggressively bored, kicking every door open like one of them might finally be hiding something interesting.

None of them were.

"Ten," I said. "There's a meeting room. We'll rest there."

I didn't think anything of saying it. Satoshi clearly didn't either - he just kept walking. But Goh stopped dead on the landing, one hand on the rail.

"Why'd you stop?" Satoshi asked, glancing back.

"Nothing," Goh said. His eyes had already found mine, though, and they didn't look like nothing.

I didn't think much of that either. Not yet.

We reached the tenth floor. The meeting room was exactly where I'd said it would be - long table, glass walls, blinds drawn over a city's worth of windows. We dropped into the chairs like our legs had only been waiting for permission.

Goh didn't sit so much as land, and the second he did, something in him cracked open, sharp enough that it caught me off guard.

"What are you hiding from us?"

I blinked. "What?"

Satoshi looked between the two of us, clearly arriving late to whatever this was. "What's the matter?"

"How did he know there'd be a meeting room on this floor?" Goh's voice was climbing fast now, aimed at Satoshi as much as at me. "We were still on twelve when he said it. I thought he was just keeping our spirits up - two more floors, hang in there. But he knew. He actually knew." He rounded on me. "How?"

"I saw the maps," I said. "There's one by every stairwell. Maybe I caught a glimpse of-"

"Don't." The word came out sharp enough that even Satoshi straightened. "I read every one of those maps, floor by floor. Each one only shows its own level - nothing else. There's no way you saw the tenth floor's layout while we were standing on the twelfth. So tell us how you know this place."

"Tell us," he said again, voice cracking on the second try. "Just tell us."

"I don't know!" It came out louder than I meant it to, raw enough that it startled even me. I dragged my voice back down, forced it level. "I don't know, okay? I wish I did. But I swear to you both - whatever this is, I'm not hiding it to hurt you. I don't have a single bad intention toward either of you. It isn't something I chose. It just happens. Like a memory walking a few steps ahead of me, and I'm only ever catching up to it."

Silence.

Goh looked at me for a long moment. Then something in his shoulders let go. "...Sorry," he muttered. "None of this makes sense, and you're the only piece of it that seems to know anything. That's terrifying, if you actually think about it."

"It is," I agreed. "Believe me. It is for me too."

It wouldn't be the last time he asked. I already knew that much. But for now, it was enough that he'd stopped looking at me like the enemy.
