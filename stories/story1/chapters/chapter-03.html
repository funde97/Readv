Entering a Whole New World

Chapter Two

The Hidden Room

Through all of it, Satoshi hadn't said a word — just watched the two of us from his chair, calm in the specific way that meant he'd have stepped between us in half a second if "tense conversation" had threatened to become something else. It hadn't. So the moment the air cleared, he went looking for something to do with his hands.

He drummed his fingers along the table, flipped a stray pen end over end, and eventually landed on a laptop near the head of the table. It was closed, but the second he opened it the screen woke up bright and fully charged, like it had been waiting for exactly this. Someone had already wired it into the big screen mounted on the wall.

There was exactly one video file on the desktop. We played it.

A man with violet hair filled the screen — which should have looked ridiculous and somehow didn't, like it belonged on him in a way I couldn't explain — older, calm in the specific way people are calm right before they tell you something that isn't calming at all. None of us recognized him. He spoke like he could see us, like the camera was a window and we were standing right on the other side of it.

"To any survivors watching this," he said, "make your way to the lift." A map flashed up behind him, a route marked in red toward the building's northeast corner. I had my phone out and a photo taken before the image had even finished loading.

Then he kept talking — about Aethelgard, the city spread out beyond our windows, about how all of this had started. A fungus, he said. Mutated, somewhere along the way, into something that behaved like a virus and a fungus at once, spreading by—

I already knew most of it. I'd read it in my own handwriting, in a diary I barely remembered writing. I let him talk anyway. Knowing something and hearing it confirmed out loud were two different kinds of relief, and right then I needed both.

What actually caught my attention wasn't the man on the screen at all.

It was the paintings.

Six of them, spaced evenly around the room, and once I noticed them I couldn't stop looking. Every single one showed the same scene from a different angle — winged figures and horned figures, locked in some kind of race, all reaching for the same object: a golden, ancient-looking harp, suspended just out of everyone's grasp. The Harp, I found myself thinking. Like it deserved the capital letter.

Something about it pulled at me the same way the stairwell had. I was on my feet and crossing to the nearest one before I'd actually decided to move, and when I pulled it away from the wall, there was a locker built into the space behind it.

"What're you—" Goh started, already crossing the room, Satoshi a half-step behind him.

I stared at the keypad. Four digits. And somewhere in the back of my head, a number surfaced — the front page of my diary, a code scrawled under my own name. I just couldn't land on it exactly. 5679. 5976. 5555. 5779. I ran through them like prayer beads.

"Try 5679," I said — the words were out before I'd really decided to say them, more confidence in my voice than I actually had.

It clicked open.

Inside: a single red button, sitting alone like it was something to be proud of.

"So what's it do?" Goh asked.

"I don't know."

He gave me a look. "You found the room, the painting, the locker, the code — and you don't know what the button does?"

"I know how that sounds! But I'm not about to guess. It could be a self-destruct switch. It could be 'close the blinds.' I'm not pressing something with those kinds of odds attached to it—"

"So do we press it or not," Goh muttered, mostly to himself, already spiraling into the exact kind of overthinking that probably came naturally to him.

Satoshi pressed it.

There was a full second of silence in which Goh and I both turned into the same kind of useless.

"You—" I managed. "You just—"

"Press— on—off—self—" Goh added, which didn't help either of us.

"If we're going to stand here arguing about whether to do something," Satoshi said, entirely unbothered, "we might as well just do it and see what happens."

"That's Satoshi for you," I muttered, mostly to myself, and mostly fondly.

The wall screen — still playing the violet-haired man, mid-sentence — split cleanly in two. On one half, he kept talking, oblivious. On the other, a new feed appeared: a small room somewhere else in the building, lit dim and gray, lined wall to wall with black tactical jackets, katanas racked in neat rows, and crates that didn't need a label to tell us what was inside them.

None of us said anything for a second.

"Okay," I said eventually, something in my chest loosening for the first time since we'd landed on that rooftop. "New plan."

It took another twenty minutes to actually find the room the camera had shown us — past a server closet, behind a door deliberately built to look like it led nowhere important. Goh went through the gear like it was a puzzle to be solved, checking every weapon twice before deciding whether he trusted it. Satoshi grabbed the first thing his hand landed on and called it good. I took what I already knew how to use, and then a little more than that, because knowing how to use something and being willing to were not always the same skill, and tonight I was going to need both.

When we were geared up, I laid the plan out as simply as I could.

"I'm immune," I said. "Or close enough to it. I've been through this before — whatever 'this' technically is — and it doesn't turn me. So I'll head west and make as much noise as I possibly can. You two go for the lift, northeast, while every set of dead eyes in this part of the city is busy following me instead."

"And the lift takes us where, exactly?" I added, half to myself. "I don't love handing my life to a box on a cable I've never once seen move."

"Probably underground," Goh said, like it was obvious. "Buildings like this usually have at least one sub-level. Parking, maintenance, something."

It was as good a guess as any, and a better one than mine. I let it become the plan.

"Quick version, before I go," I said. "They're not alive. Whatever's walking around down there used to be a person, but the fungus is driving now, and the person isn't home anymore. It spreads through bites, so don't let anything with teeth get close enough to use them. They track sound and blood before anything else, so move quiet, and if you bleed, clean it fast. And the longer one's been dead, the less 'alive' it acts — which means cutting the head off won't necessarily stop it. There's nothing left in there to kill. It was already gone before you ever got close."

Goh's face had gone pale somewhere in the middle of that, but he nodded.

Satoshi just clapped a hand on my shoulder. "Stay alive," he said. "We'll be waiting for you."

I didn't trust myself to say anything back that wouldn't crack halfway through, so I didn't try. I took the stairs down to the underground garage instead, where a single motorcycle sat under a layer of dust. Someone had scratched BULLET into the tank a long time ago, and the name had stuck.

I swung a leg over it and didn't look back.
